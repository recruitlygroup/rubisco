# Heifer Rearing Targets, Live Weight Benchmarks & First-Calving Target Age

Rearing youngstock efficiently ensures heifers enter the milking herd at 24 months of age with sufficient frame size and body condition to maximize lifetime milk solids yield. Achieving key live-weight targets at 6, 12, 15, and 22 months of age directly impacts first-lactation fertility, survival rate, and overall herd replacement economics.

---

## 1. Key Weight Benchmarks relative to Mature Live Weight

Heifer growth targets are best expressed as a percentage of the mature herd's live weight (typically measured at third or fourth lactation).

[ 30% Mature Weight (6 Months) ] ---> [ 50% Mature Weight (12 Months) ] ---> [ 60% Target Breeding Weight (15 Months) ] ---> [ 90% Calving Weight (24 Months) ]


### Live-Weight Growth Targets

* **Weaning Target (8–10 Weeks):** Double the birth weight (typically **70 to 85 kg** for Holstein-Friesian / Crossbred calves).
* **6-Month Target:** Achieve **30%** of mature live weight (e.g., 165–180 kg for a 550 kg mature cow).
* **12-Month Target:** Achieve **50%** of mature live weight (e.g., 275–300 kg).
* **15-Month Target (Mating Onset):** Achieve **60%** of mature live weight (e.g., 330–360 kg) to ensure pubertal cycling and high first-service conception rates.
* **22 to 24-Month Target (Pre-Calving):** Achieve **85% to 90%** of mature live weight (e.g., 470–500 kg pre-calving) with a Target Body Condition Score (BCS) of **3.0 to 3.25** (5.0 scale).

---

## 2. Average Daily Gain (ADG) Requirements & Feeding Strategies

Maintaining a consistent Average Daily Gain (ADG) throughout the rearing period avoids under-development or over-fattening, which damages mammary gland parenchymal tissue.

| Rearing Phase | ADG Target Range | Nutritional Strategy | Risk Factors |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Weaning to 6 Months** | **0.75 – 0.85 kg/day** | High-energy starter meal + lush pasture / high-protein silage | Under-feeding causes stunted skeletal frames |
| **6 to 12 Months** | **0.70 – 0.80 kg/day** | High-quality pasture + balanced fiber supplementation | Over-fattening deposits fat in mammary glands |
| **Mating to Pre-Calving** | **0.65 – 0.75 kg/day** | Controlled pasture intake / balanced forage-based TMR | Rapid weight drops cause abortion or embryonic loss |

---

## 3. Regular Weighing, Grouping & Feed Redistribution

Relying on visual estimation leads to underestimating heifer weights, resulting in light heifers at mating and increased calving difficulty (dystocia).

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                   YOUNGSTOCK WEIGHING PROTOCOL                    |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Monitoring Phase  | Action Threshold & Operational Protocol       |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Weighing Frequency| Weigh all heifers every 60 to 90 days         |
| Banding / Grouping| Draft into 2 groups: "On-Target" & "Light"    |
| Preferential Feed | Feed "Light" group extra concentrates/pasture |
| Mating Cut-Off    | Exclude heifers <55% mature weight at mating  |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+

## 4. The Economic Impact of First-Calving Target Age (24 Months)

Calving heifers down at 24 months maximizes milk production return on investment compared to delayed calving at 26–30 months.

* **Lower Rearing Costs:** Reduces feed, housing, and labor expenses by **15% to 20%** per replacement heifer.
* **Higher Lifetime Yield:** 24-month heifers spend a higher proportion of their total farm lifespan actively producing milk.
* **Better Herd Survival:** Heifers that enter the herd on target exhibit better 6-week in-calf rates and lower involuntary culling in early lactations.

Executing regular weight monitoring and implementing targeted nutrition ensures youngstock reach key milestones, delivering robust, high-performing first-lactation cows.
