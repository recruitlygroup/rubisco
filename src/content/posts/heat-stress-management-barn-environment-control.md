# Heat Stress Management & Barn Environment Control: Safeguarding Yields in Commercial Dairy Systems

Heat stress is a major environmental threat to commercial dairy profitability, causing sharp drops in daily dry matter intake (DMI), reduced milk yields, impaired conception rates, and elevated somatic cell counts. Modern high-producing dairy cows generate significant metabolic heat, experiencing physiological stress at lower ambient temperatures than most farm managers anticipate—especially when humidity levels are high.

At CTEVT-affiliated agricultural training centers, candidate instruction emphasizes proactive climate monitoring, ventilation management, and practical cooling protocols to maintain cow comfort and herd productivity through warm seasons.

---

## 1. Temperature-Humidity Index (THI) & Stress Thresholds

Monitoring temperature alone is insufficient; relative humidity amplifies heat retention in cattle. Training candidates learn to monitor the Temperature-Humidity Index (THI) to trigger cooling interventions before clinical stress symptoms appear.

[ THI > 68: Mild Stress ] ---> [ Soaking & Fan Activation ] ---> [ DMI & Water Audit ] ---> [ Rumen Buffer Adjustments ]


### THI Operational Benchmarks

* **Mild Stress ($\text{THI } 68–71$):** Respiration rates rise above 60 breaths per minute. Candidates activate soaking cycles in holding yards and housing feed alleys.
* **Moderate Stress ($\text{THI } 72–79$):** Drooling, reduced rumination, and significant DMI decline. Continuous fan ventilation and high-frequency soaking protocols are enforced.
* **Severe Stress ($\text{THI } \ge 80$):** Open-mouth panting and crowding around water troughs. Emergency cooling and veterinary triage protocols are initiated.

## 2. Active Cooling & Environmental Control Systems

Combining air velocity with intermittent soaking maximizes heat dissipation through evaporative cooling off the cow's skin.

| Cooling Zone | System Hardware | Student Operational Protocol |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Milking Parlour Holding Yard** | Overhead high-volume fans & coarse spray nozzles | Runs 30-second soak / 4-minute fan cycle to cool cows prior to milking |
| **Freestall Feed Alley** | Soaker lines positioned above feed barrier | Activates automated soaking triggered by cow proximity and THI sensors |
| **Resting & Cubicle Area** | High-volume low-speed (HVLS) roof fans | Maintains continuous $2–3\text{ m/s}$ airflow across lying surfaces |
