# Metabolic Disease Prevention & Transition Cow Nutrition Protocols

The transition period—spanning three weeks prior to calving to three weeks post-calving—is the most critical phase in the lactation cycle. Proper transition management minimizes metabolic disorders, supports a smooth onset of lactation, and sets the foundation for high herd peak milk production and subsequent reproductive success.

---

## 1. The Biological Challenge of the Transition Phase

At calving, a dairy cow's demand for calcium and energy spikes dramatically within hours to support colostrum and milk production, while dry matter intake (DMI) naturally declines.

[ Pre-Calving DMI Drop ] ---> [ Calving Energy Spike ] ---> [ Negative Energy Balance ] ---> [ Metabolic Risk Window ]


### Key Physiological Changes

* **Calcium Demand Surge:** A high-yielding cow requires **up to 4 times more calcium** on the day of calving than during late pregnancy to support milk synthesis.
* **Negative Energy Balance (NEB):** DMI drops by **20% to 30%** in the final days before calving, forcing the cow to mobilize body fat reserves to bridge the energy deficit.

---

## 2. Dietary Cation-Anion Difference (DCAD) & Milk Fever Control

Manipulating the Dietary Cation-Anion Difference (DCAD) in pre-calving rations alters systemic blood pH, stimulating the cow's hormonal mechanisms to mobilize calcium from bone reserves.

| Nutrient Group | Key Minerals Included | Pre-Calving Target Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cations (+)** | Potassium ($K^+$), Sodium ($Na^+$) | Keep low in pre-calving rations to prevent tissue unresponsiveness |
| **Anions (-)** | Chloride ($Cl^-$), Sulfur ($S^{2-}$) | Add via anionic salts to create mild subclinical metabolic acidosis |

### Practical DCAD Implementation Rules

* **Target Urinary pH:** Test urine pH of pre-calving cows weekly. Target a range of **6.0 to 6.5** for Holsteins (indicating effective anionic salt loading).
* **Potassium Limitation:** Avoid applying high-potassium slurry or effluent to dry cow paddocks; keep forage potassium levels below **1.5% DM** prior to calving.

---

## 3. Ketosis Monitoring & Rumen Adjustment Protocols

When negative energy balance becomes too severe, the liver converts mobilized fatty acids into ketone bodies (*beta-hydroxybutyrate / BHB*), leading to subclinical or clinical ketosis.

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                   KETOSIS INTERVENTION THRESHOLDS                 |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Blood BHB Level   | Diagnosis & Action Required                   |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| < 1.2 mmol/L      | Normal metabolic status                       |
| 1.2 – 2.9 mmol/L  | Subclinical Ketosis; oral drench with glycol   |
| >= 3.0 mmol/L     | Clinical Ketosis; contact vet for IV dextrose |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+


### Prevention Strategies

1. **Starch Transition:** Introduce small amounts of the lactation concentrate ration (**1–2 kg daily**) 10 to 14 days pre-calving to adapt rumen microbes to starch digestion.
2. **Body Condition Targets:** Ensure cows enter the dry period at a Body Condition Score (BCS) of **3.0 to 3.25**. Over-conditioned cows (BCS >3.5) experience a much sharper drop in DMI and higher rates of liver ketosis.

---

## 4. Post-Calving Recovery & Early Lactation Care

Immediate post-calving care ensures cows resume normal feed and water intake quickly, protecting them against displaced abomasum (DA) and retained fetal membranes.

* **Warm Fluid Drenching:** Offer **20 to 30 liters of warm water** containing electrolytes and calcium immediately post-calving to fill the empty abdominal cavity and rehydrate the cow.
* **Unrestricted High-Quality Forage:** Provide free-choice, highly digestible forage alongside fresh water to encourage immediate intake post-calving.

By controlling DCAD, monitoring blood BHB levels, and supporting immediate post-calving intake, farm teams reduce metabolic disease incidences and protect herd longevity.
