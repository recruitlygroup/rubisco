# Calf Scours Management, Electrolyte Therapy & Fluid Rehydration Protocols

Neonatal calf diarrhea (scours) remains a leading cause of mortality in youngstock rearing systems. Caused by viral (*Rotavirus*, *Coronavirus*), bacterial (*E. coli*, *Salmonella*), or protozoal (*Cryptosporidium parvum*) pathogens, severe scours leads to rapid fluid loss, metabolic acidosis, and electrolyte depletion. Implementing early diagnosis, accurate dehydration assessment, and structured fluid therapy protects calf welfare and speeds recovery.

---

## 1. Pathogen Identification & Clinical Diagnostic Workflow

Identifying scours early prevents severe dehydration and reduces transmission across group housing pens.

[ Daily Fecal & Vigor Check ] ---> [ Isolation & Temperature ] ---> [ Dehydration Assessment ] ---> [ Targeted Electrolyte Plan ]


### Key Diagnostic Markers

* **Watery Diarrhea:** Pale yellow, white, or bloody loose feces indicating mucosal damage and malabsorption.
* **Depressed Demeanor:** Drooping ears, sunken eyes, dry muzzle, and delayed suckle reflex.
* **Rectal Temperature:** Normal range is **38.5°C to 39.5°C**. Temperatures above **39.5°C** indicate active systemic infection requiring veterinary review, while temperatures below **38.0°C** signal severe hypothermia and shock.

---

## 2. Assessing Dehydration Severity & Calculating Fluid Deficits

Determining the percentage of dehydration dictates whether oral rehydration therapy (ORT) is sufficient or if intravenous (IV) fluid intervention is required.

| Dehydration Level | Physical Symptoms | Skin Tent Duration | Sunken Eye Distance | Required Action Protocol |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mild (5% – 6%)** | Active calf, strong suckle, slightly dry muzzle | **2 – 6 seconds** | Eyes flush with orbit | Oral Electrolytes (2 L) post-milk feed |
| **Moderate (7% – 9%)** | Lethargic, weak suckle, dry gums, cold ears | **6 – 10 seconds** | 2 mm – 4 mm gap | Oral Electrolytes (2 L – 3 L) twice daily |
| **Severe (10%–12%)** | Recumbent (unable to stand), no suckle, cold skin | **>10 seconds** | >6 mm deep gap | **Emergency IV Fluids** (Sodium Bicarbonate / Ringer's) |

---

## 3. Oral Electrolyte Formulation & Administration Standards

Effective oral electrolyte solutions (OES) must supply sodium, alkalinizing agents (bicarbonate, acetate, or citrate), and glycine or glucose to drive sodium absorption across the intestinal wall.

|                  FLUID RECOVERY CALCULATION EXAMPLE               |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Calf Weight = 40 kg | Dehydration = 8%                            |
|                                                                   |
| 1. Deficit Volume: 40 kg x 0.08 = 3.2 Liters                      |

### Feeding Management Rules

* **DO NOT Stop Milk Feeding:** Continue offering normal liquid milk or replacer feeds in separate meals (at least 2 hours apart from electrolytes). Milk provides essential energy to maintain body weight and repair damaged gut epithelium.
* **Alkalinizing Agents:** Use acetate-buffered electrolytes when feeding milk, as high bicarbonate levels can interfere with abomasal milk curd formation.

---

## 4. Biosecurity & Pen Sanitation Controls

Containing infectious scour outbreaks requires strict hygiene routines to prevent cross-contamination across calf groups.

* **Sick Pen Isolation:** Move scouring calves to individual isolation pens immediately upon detection.
* **Dedicated Equipment:** Use separate feeding buckets and teats for sick calves; always feed and treat healthy calves **first** before handling isolated animals.
* **Disinfection:** Clean pens thoroughly using disinfectants effective against oocysts (e.g., amine-based products certified for *Cryptosporidium* control).

Early rehydration therapy combined with strict isolation protocols reduces calf mortality and maintains youngstock target growth curves.
