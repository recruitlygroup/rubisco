# Soil Health, Nutrient Management Planning & Fertilizer Efficiency

Soil fertility is the foundational driver of pasture production and herd carry capacity. Implementing a structured Nutrient Management Plan (NMP) ensures that nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and sulfur are applied at precise agronomic rates, maximizing grass growth while preventing environmental leaching and soil acidification.

---

## 1. Soil Testing Protocols & Key Chemical Metrics

Regular soil sampling provides accurate baseline data on nutrient availability across different farm soil types and management blocks.

[ Strategic Core Sampling (0–75 mm) ] ---> [ Lab Analysis (Olsen P, MAF K, pH) ] ---> [ Precision Fertilizer Plan ]


### Essential Soil Test Parameters

* **Soil pH:** Target **5.8 to 6.2** for mineral soils. Low pH impairs root development and locks up essential macro-nutrients.
* **Olsen Phosphorus (P):** Target **20 to 30 mg/L** depending on soil volcanic content and stocking rate.
* **Quicktest Potassium (K):** Target **5 to 8** for dairy pastures to support clover growth and plant cell wall strength.
* **Organic Sulfur ($S\text{-}SO_4$):** Target **10 to 12 mg/kg** to prevent amino acid deficiencies in fast-growing pasture.

---

## 2. Soil pH Optimization & Liming Strategies

Applying agricultural lime increases soil pH, reduces toxic aluminum levels, and improves soil microbial activity responsible for organic matter breakdown.

| Soil pH Range | Action Required | Application Rate Benchmark |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **< 5.5** | High Priority Liming | Apply **2.5 to 5.0 tonnes/ha** split across seasons |
| **5.5 – 5.7** | Maintenance Liming | Apply **1.0 to 2.0 tonnes/ha** |
| **5.8 – 6.2** | Optimum Target Zone | Monitor every 2–3 years; no immediate lime needed |

---

## 3. Nitrogen (N) Application Efficiency & Loss Reduction

Nitrogen fuels rapid leaf expansion and dry matter accumulation, but excessive or poorly timed applications risk nitrate leaching and nitrous oxide emissions.

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                     NITROGEN APPLICATION RULES                    |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Variable          | Standard Operating Guideline                  |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Single Application| Apply 25 to 40 kg N/ha per dressing           |
| Soil Temperature  | Apply only when soil temp is above 6°C at 9 AM|
| Soil Moisture     | Avoid applying to saturated or waterlogged soil|
| Annual Cap        | Stay within regional regulatory N caps (e.g. 190 kg N/ha)|
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+


### Timing Application with Pasture Growth

* **Post-Grazing Window:** Apply nitrogen immediately after stock leave a paddock (**within 1–3 days**) so regrowing tillers take up the mineral nitrogen rapidly.
* **Inhibitors:** Use urease inhibitors on solid urea to prevent ammonia volatilization during dry, warm application periods.

---

## 4. Integrated Nutrient Cycling & Fertilizer Records

To optimize total fertilizer expenditure, synthetic fertilizer inputs must be integrated with organic effluent applications.

* **Effluent Block Credit:** Account for nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium delivered via farm effluent, reducing synthetic inputs on those paddocks accordingly.
* **Annual Nutrient Budgeting:** Track inputs versus outputs (milk, meat, fodder removed) using software models to prevent long-term soil nutrient mining or excessive buildup.

Maintaining balanced soil fertility through disciplined testing, target liming, and strategic nitrogen applications secures high annual pasture yields and supports long-term farm sustainability.
