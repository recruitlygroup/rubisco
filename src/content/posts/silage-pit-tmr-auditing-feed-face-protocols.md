# Silage Pit Management, Total Mixed Ration (TMR) Auditing & Feed Face Protocols

Delivering a balanced Total Mixed Ration (TMR) requires more than simply formulating a diet on paper; it demands strict operational precision at the silage pit face and mixing wagon. Controlling aerobic spoilage during feed-out and auditing physical TMR mix consistency prevents sorting, stabilizes rumen pH, and optimizes daily dry matter intake across the herd.

---

## 1. Silage Pit Face Management & Aerobic Spoilage Prevention

Exposing high-quality conserved forage to oxygen triggers secondary yeast and mold multiplication, rapidly generating heat and degrading Metabolisable Energy (ME) values.

[ Clean Face Cut ] ---> [ Controlled Daily Advance ] ---> [ Immediate Feed Loading ] ---> [ Face Protection ]


### Essential Pit Face Protocols

* **Face Defacing:** Use a block cutter, shear grab, or defacer to maintain a tight, smooth, and vertical pit face. Avoid pulling or lifting silage from the bottom up, which introduces air deep into the unharvested stack.
* **Advance Speed Targets:** Remove at least **15 to 20 cm** of face depth daily during winter and **30 cm** during warm summer months to outrun oxygen penetration.
* **Plastic Overhang Control:** Keep the top plastic cover weighted tightly against the active face using gravel bags to prevent air from entering along the top edge.

## 2. Penn State Particle Size Separator (PSPS) TMR Auditing

Monotonous physical particle size distribution ensures adequate effective fiber (peNDF) to stimulate rumination while preventing cows from sorting out long forage particles in favor of fine concentrate grains.

| Penn State Sieve Level | Sieve Mesh Size | Target Ration Percentage | Primary Biological Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Top Sieve** | **19 mm** | **2% to 8%** | Stimulates cud-chewing and salivary buffer production |
| **Middle Sieve** | **8 mm** | **30% to 50%** | Provides digestible fiber; maintains rumen mat density |
| **Lower Sieve** | **4 mm** | **10% to 20%** | Captures medium particles and forage fragments |
| **Bottom Pan** | Solid Pan | **< 30% to 40%** | Delivers rapid fermentable starch and protein sources |

## 3. Mixer Wagon Operations & Loading Sequence

Incorrect loading sequences or over-mixing breaks down structural fiber, leading to subclinical ruminal acidosis (SARA), while under-mixing promotes feed sorting.

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                     TMR LOADING & MIX SEQUENCE                    |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Step              | Standard Ingredient Order                     |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| 1. Long Fiber     | Add dry hay/straw; chop to target length      |
| 2. Silages        | Add maize, grass, or whole-crop silage        |
| 3. Liquids/By-products| Add liquid molasses, water, or wet feeds  |
| 4. Concentrates   | Add protein meals, minerals, and grain mix    |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+


### Mixing Audit Parameters

* **Mix Time Limit:** Avoid mixing longer than **3 to 5 minutes** after the final ingredient is loaded into a vertical mixer to preserve effective fiber structure.
* **Blades & Knives:** Inspect and replace worn mixing tub knives quarterly to prevent shredded, ragged forage cuts.

---

## 4. Feed Alley Management & Refusal Auditing

Maintaining consistent access to fresh, palatable feed drives daily dry matter intake and prevents aggressive standing behavior at the feed barrier.

* **Target Refusal Level:** Aim for a **2% to 4% refusal rate** post-24-hour feeding period. Zero refusals indicate that cows are being under-fed, restricting milk output.
* **Feed Push-Up Frequency:** Push feed closer to the barrier **4 to 6 times daily**—especially during the first 4 hours post-fresh feed delivery—to encourage continuous intake.

By controlling face exposure, auditing particle size distribution, and standardizing loading routines, farm teams safeguard rumen stability and maximize total feed conversion efficiency.
