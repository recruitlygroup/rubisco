# Strategic Dry Cow Therapy, Selective Treatment Protocols & Drying-Off Hygiene

The dry period is a crucial phase in the lactation cycle for repairing udder tissue and clearing subclinical intramammary infections. Implementing rigorous drying-off protocols—combining antimicrobial treatments where indicated with internal teat sealants—protects cows from new environmental infections while reducing overall antibiotic reliance on farm.

---

## 1. Selective Dry Cow Therapy (SDCT) vs. Blanket Treatment

Selective Dry Cow Therapy (SDCT) targets antibiotic administration exclusively to cows with confirmed bacterial infections, applying an internal teat sealant (ITS) alone to healthy uninfected cows.

[ Individual SCC & CMT Screen ] ---> [ High SCC / History: Antibiotic + Sealant ] ---> [ Low SCC / Clean: Sealant Only ]


### Selection Criteria Guidelines

* **Healthy Cow Threshold (Teat Sealant Only):** Individual Somatic Cell Count (SCC) consistently **<150,000 cells/mL** across all milk recordings in the current lactation, with no clinical mastitis episodes.
* **Treated Cow Threshold (Antibiotic + Sealant):** Individual SCC **>150,000 cells/mL** or any recorded clinical mastitis event during the season.

---

## 2. Sterile Application Protocol for Internal Teat Sealants & Tubes

Faulty administration during drying-off can accidentally introduce environmental pathogens (*E. coli*, *Pseudomonas*) deep into the teat cistern, leading to severe illness or death.

| Phase | Standard Operating Procedure | Critical Control Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Preparation** | Milk out quarter completely; wear fresh nitrile gloves | Work in clean, draft-free environment |
| **Disinfection** | Scrub teat end orifice thoroughly with 70% alcohol wipes | Clean far teats first, then near teats |
| **Infusion** | Insert nozzle tip partially into the teat canal; infuse slowly | Infuse near teats first, then far teats |
| **Post-Dipping** | Apply heavy barrier post-dip immediately after infusion | Do **not** massage teat after sealant infusion |

---

## 3. Post-Drying-Off Paddock & Herd Management

The first **10 to 14 days post-drying-off** represent a high-risk window as teat plugs are actively forming and udders remain full of milk.

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  DRY HORIZON MANAGEMENT CHECKLIST                 |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Monitoring Area   | Operational Requirement                       |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Environment       | Move dried-off cows directly to dry, clean pastures|
| Visual Inspection | Check dry herd daily for swollen or hot quarters|
| Feed Management   | Restrict high-starch grains to reduce milk drive|
| No Hand Insertion | Never strip out dry cows unless treating infection|
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+


---

## 4. Calving Prep & Teat Sealant Removal

Proper management at calving ensures internal teat sealants are stripped out thoroughly before the cow joins the main milking herd.

* **Manual Stripping at Calving:** Hand-strip **10 to 12 strong streams** from each quarter into a bucket during the first feed to completely remove the heavy bismuth subnitrate sealant plug.
* **Protect the Cluster:** Never allow the milking cluster to draw teat sealant into the milk line or plate cooler to prevent filter clogging and milk quality rejection.

By standardizing drying-off hygiene and employing selective dry cow protocols, farm managers protect quarter health, maintain high milk quality compliance, and optimize herd performance for the upcoming season.
