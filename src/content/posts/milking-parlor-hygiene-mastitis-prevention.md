# Milking Parlor Hygiene, Equipment Operations & Mastitis Control Protocols

Delivering top-grade milk while maintaining low **Somatic Cell Counts (SCC)** requires absolute consistency in milking routines and parlour sanitation. Whether operating a rotary parlour, a herringbone shed, or a side-by-side system, standardizing herd handling, teat preparation, and equipment maintenance directly protects animal welfare and bulk tank revenue.

---

## 1. The Physiology of Milk Let-Down & Pre-Milking Workflow

Achieving fast, clean, and complete milking relies on harvesting the cow's natural hormonal response. Stimulating the teat triggers the release of **oxytocin** from the pituitary gland, causing alveolar myoepithelial cells to contract and release milk into the cistern.

[ Teat Clean & Stimulate ] ---> [ Oxytocin Release ] ---> [ Peak Milk Flow (60–90s) ] ---> [ Cluster Attachment ]

### Step-by-Step Pre-Milking Routine

1. **Forestripping & Visual Inspection:**
   * Strip **2–3 streams** of milk from each quarter onto a black strip cup or rubber mat.
   * Inspect thoroughly for *clots, watery milk, or discoloration* indicating clinical mastitis.
   * *Never strip onto gloved hands or stall floors* to prevent cross-contamination.

2. **Teat Disinfection (Pre-Dipping):**
   * Apply an approved fast-acting pre-dip (e.g., iodine or hydrogen peroxide-based disinfectant) ensuring **at least 75% coverage** of the teat barrel.
   * Allow a minimum **contact time of 30 seconds** for effective pathogen destruction.

3. **Wiping & Drying:**
   * Wipe each teat firmly with a **single-use paper towel** or individual sanitized cloth.
   * Focus on the *teat end (orifice)* to remove lingering organic matter and disinfectant chemical residues before cluster attachment.

4. **Timely Cluster Attachment:**
   * Attach teat cups within **60 to 90 seconds** of initial stimulation to maximize oxytocin response and lower total unit-on time.

## 2. Parlour Equipment Operation & Vacuum Line Standards

Improper vacuum levels or degraded liner rubbers cause teat-end hyperkeratosis, liner slip, and tissue trauma—drastically increasing mastitis risk.

### Key Operational Benchmarks

* **System Vacuum Level:** Maintain stable vacuum pressure at the gauge:
  * *Low-line systems:* **38–40 kPa** (11.2–11.8 inHg)
  * *High-line systems:* **48–50 kPa** (14.2–14.8 inHg)
* **Pulsation Rate & Ratio:**
  * Standard pulse rate: **60 cycles per minute**.
  * Pulsation ratio: **60:40** (milk phase to collapse/rest phase) to ensure adequate blood circulation in the teat tip.
* **Liner Maintenance Schedule:**
  * Replace rubber liners after **2,500 milkings** or 6 months (whichever comes first).
  * Replace silicone liners after **5,000–8,000 milkings** depending on manufacturer specifications.

## 3. Post-Milking Sanitation & Teat Protection

Immediately after cluster removal, the teat canal remains dilated for **30 to 45 minutes**, leaving the udder highly vulnerable to environmental bacteria like *E. coli* and *Streptococcus uberis*.

### Mandatory Post-Milking Actions

* **Post-Dipping (Teat Sealing):**
  * Dip teats immediately upon cluster removal with a barrier film disinfectant or emollient-rich solution.
  * *Coverage target:* **100% of the teat skin** that made contact with the liner.
* **Feed Allocation Timing:**
  * Ensure fresh feed or silage is immediately accessible in the loafing area post-milking.
  * This encourages cows to **stand upright for at least 45 minutes**, allowing sphincter muscles to seal before animals lie down in cubicles or pasture.

## 4. Sanitation Cycles for Plant & Bulk Tank CIP

Clean-in-Place (CIP) regimes prevent milkstone buildup and thermoduric bacteria growth inside milk lines and cooling tanks.

| Wash Phase | Water Temp | Chemical / Agent | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Cold Rinse** | Lukewarm (35–45°C) | Potable Fresh Water | Flushes out residual milk solids (prevents protein baking). |
| **2. Main Hot Wash** | Start 85°C / End >60°C | Chlorinated Alkaline Detergent | Dissolves milk fats and protein films. |
| **3. Acid Rinse** | Cold / Warm Water | Phosphoric / Nitric Acid Solution | Removes mineral deposits (*milkstone*) and lowers pH (run 2–3 times/week). |
| **4. Sanitizing Flush** | Cold Water | Peracetic Acid or Chlorine Solution | Kills remaining micro-organisms prior to next milking session. |

---

## 5. Early Mastitis Identification & SCC Thresholds

Managing Somatic Cell Count keeps bulk tank performance within premium pay bands and reduces involuntary culling.

### Health Threshold Guidance

* **Individual Cow Target:** **<150,000 cells/mL** (indicates an uninfected udder).
* **Subclinical Mastitis:** **>250,000 cells/mL** (requires California Mastitis Test (CMT) evaluation to isolate infected quarter).
* **Clinical Action Plan:**
  1. Isolate the affected cow immediately and milk her last or into a dump bucket.
  2. Take a sterile milk sample for bacterial culturing/sensitivity testing before administering antibiotic treatments.
  3. Log treatment records strictly according to veterinary drug withholding period regulations (for both milk and meat).

By enforcing clear pre-milking routines, maintaining precise equipment parameters, and executing strict CIP sanitation, modern farm teams safeguard milk quality, reduce treatment expenses, and support overall herd durability.
