# Dairy Herd Health Care, Preventive Disease Control & Livestock Management

Maintaining a healthy, highly productive dairy herd requires proactive health protocols rather than reactive treatments. Implementing systematic health monitoring, balanced nutrition, and robust biosecurity measures protects livestock welfare, reduces culling rates, and ensures consistent milk production across every stage of the lactation cycle.

---

## 1. Body Condition Scoring (BCS) & Transition Cow Management

Managing energy balance during the transition period—3 weeks before calving to 3 weeks post-calving—is critical to preventing metabolic disorders and optimizing reproductive performance.

[ Dry Period (BCS 3.0-3.25) ] ---> [ Calving (BCS 3.0) ] ---> [ Early Lactation (Max Loss 0.5 BCS) ]


### Target Body Condition Score (BCS) Benchmarks (1–5 Scale)

* **At Calving:** Target **3.0 – 3.25**. Cows carrying excess fat (BCS >3.5) are at high risk for fatty liver syndrome and ketosis, while thin cows (BCS <2.75) struggle with low peak milk yields and poor conception rates.
* **Early Lactation Peak:** Target **2.5 – 2.75**. Minimize condition loss to **no more than 0.5 to 0.75 units** post-calving to maintain peak fertility.
* **Late Lactation & Drying Off:** Target **3.0**. Rebuild energy reserves gradually using forage-based diets before the dry period begins.

## 2. Preventing & Managing Common Metabolic Disorders

Metabolic diseases occurring around calving drastically impact animal health and milk solids output. Standardizing early identification and intervention protocols reduces veterinary intervention costs.

| Disorder | Primary Root Cause | Early Warning Signs | Immediate Action / Prevention |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Subclinical Hypocalcemia (Milk Fever)** | Acute blood calcium deficit post-calving | Cold ears, muscle tremors, wobbly gait, unsteadiness | Administer oral calcium boluses post-calving; balance DCAD in dry cow rations |
| **Ketosis (Acetonaemia)** | Negative Energy Balance (NEB) driving fat mobilization | Rapid weight drop, sweet acetone breath, refusal of grain | Drench with propylene glycol; feed high-energy digestible carbohydrates |
| **Ruminal Acidosis** | Excessive rapidly fermentable carbohydrates / low fiber | Loose bright yellow manure, reduced cud-chewing, drop in fat % | Maintain effective fiber (>30% NDF); include dietary buffers (sodium bicarbonate) |

## 3. Lameness Prevention & Hoof Care Protocols

Lameness is a major welfare and economic issue that directly reduces daily feed intake and reproductive performance.

### Essential Hoof Health Actions

1. **Regular Structural Mobility Scoring:**
   * Walk behind the herd weekly to observe track and spinal curvature.
   * *Score 0/1:* Normal gait.
   * *Score 2/3:* Mild to severe lameness needing immediate foot trimming and treatment in a crush.

2. **Footbath Management:**
   * Run the herd through a **2%–5% Copper Sulfate** or **Formalin footbath** twice weekly to control infectious digital dermatitis.
   * Maintain a footbath length of at least **3 meters** to ensure each hoof receives at least two full submersions.

3. **Track & Laneway Maintenance:**
   * Ensure raceways and farm tracks are free from sharp gravel, well-drained, and surfaced with clay or rubber mats near the shed entry.

## 4. Biosecurity, Vaccination & Quarantine Frameworks

Preventing infectious pathogens from entering the farm platform is far more cost-effective than managing an outbreak.

### Biosecurity Checklist

* **Quarantine New Stock:** Isolate all newly arrived livestock or returning cattle in a dedicated quarantine paddock for **at least 21 days** before herd integration.
* **Diagnostic Screening:** Test incoming animals for *Bovine Viral Diarrhea (BVD)*, *Leptospirosis*, and *Johne’s Disease*.
* **Vaccination Schedule:** Administer core herd vaccines (e.g., 7-in-1 or 5-in-1 vaccines covering Leptospirosis and Clostridial diseases) annually prior to mating and calving seasons.
* **Visitor Sanitation:** Enforce footbath usage and boot washing stations for all visiting service technicians, vets, and transport drivers.

By combining structured condition scoring, preventive nutrition, mobility tracking, and strict biosecurity routines, modern dairy operations safeguard animal welfare while maintaining long-term herd profitability.
