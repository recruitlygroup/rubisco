# Silage Quality, Forage Conservation & Feed Preservation Protocols

High-quality conserved forage acts as an essential buffer during seasonal pasture deficits. Conserving grass or maize silage at peak nutritional value minimizes dry matter losses, maintains Metabolisable Energy (ME) levels, and ensures consistent herd production when live pasture growth slows.

---

## 1. Mowing Timelines & Dry Matter Target Thresholds

Harvesting at the right plant growth stage balances total yield per hectare with crude protein and digestible energy content.

[ Pre-Cut Inspection (Leaf Stage) ] ---> [ Rapid Field Wilting ] ---> [ Target Dry Matter Test ] ---> [ Chop & Pit ]


### Optimum Dry Matter (DM) Benchmarks

* **Grass Silage (Pit/Bunker):** Target **30% to 35% DM**. Mowing at the pre-heading stage (2.5 to 3 leaves) maximizes energy ($>11.5\text{ MJ ME/kg DM}$) and digestibility.
* **Baled Silage (Wrapped):** Target **40% to 50% DM**. Higher dry matter prevents bale distortion and reduces mold risk under plastic film.
* **Maize Silage:** Target **32% to 35% DM** whole-plant content, harvested when the starch kernel milk line sits at **50% to 60%** (2/3 milk line down the kernel).

---

## 2. Compaction Density & Anaerobic Fermentation

The primary goal of silage making is to rapidly drop the pH to **3.8–4.2** through anaerobic lactic acid fermentation. Oxygen is the primary enemy of silage quality, promoting yeast and mold growth.

| Processing Stage | Standard Operating Parameter | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chop Length** | **15–20 mm** (Grass) / **10–15 mm** (Maize) | Ensures optimal consolidation without ruining rumen fiber effect |
| **Layer Thickness** | **150–200 mm** maximum per pass | Allows heavy tractor tires to press out air pockets completely |
| **Compaction Weight** | **Tractor Weight = 25%–30%** of hourly delivery rate | Achieves target density of **>220 kg DM/m³** |

---

## 3. Pit Sealing, Barrier Films & Inoculants

Rapid sealing immediately after packing stops plant respiration and preserves water-soluble carbohydrates.

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                     PIT SEALING BEST PRACTICES                    |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Layer             | Specification & Role                          |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Oxygen Barrier    | 40-micron vacuum film that clings to surface  |
| Heavy Poly Sheet  | 150-micron UV-stabilized primary plastic cover|
| Weight Protection | Woven gravel bags or full tire-to-tire cover  |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+


### Inoculant Application

* Apply tested lactic acid bacterial inoculants (*Lactobacillus plantarum*) at the chopper or pit face.
* Accelerated fermentation drops pH rapidly, protecting plant proteins from breakdown into unwanted ammonia.

---

## 4. Face Management & Feed-Out Execution

Spoilage during feed-out occurs when air penetrates the open pit face, triggering secondary aerobic fermentation.

* **Block Cutting:** Use a sharp shear-grab or block-cutter to keep the pit face clean, vertical, and tightly packed.
* **Feed-Out Rate:** Advance the working face by at least **15 cm daily in winter** and **30 cm daily in summer** to outrun air penetration.
* **Clean Face:** Never pull down loose material from the top of the face that will not be fed within the same 12-hour period.

Executing clean harvesting, dense consolidation, and strict face management protects conserved feed investments and keeps dietary energy stable year-round.
