# Pasture Irrigation Systems, Center Pivot Management & Water Application Efficiency

In low-rainfall or drought-prone dairy regions, supplementary pasture irrigation is critical for maintaining consistent dry matter yield throughout summer and autumn. Optimizing irrigation hardware—specifically center pivots, pod systems, and traveling irrigators—combined with precision soil moisture monitoring ensures maximum grass growth per millimeter of applied water while avoiding runoff and deep drainage losses.

---

## 1. Comparing Irrigation Delivery Hardware

Selecting and operating the correct irrigation system depends on farm topography, soil infiltration capacity, capital investment, and labor availability.

| System Type | Application Efficiency | Labor Requirement | Key Operational Parameters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Center Pivot** | **85% – 95%** | Very Low | Low pressure (1.0–2.0 bar); ideal for large, flat, uniform blocks |

## 2. Soil Moisture Monitoring & Irrigation Scheduling

Irrigating without real-time soil moisture data leads to under-watering (pasture stress and lower DM response) or over-watering (nutrient leaching and saturated soil conditions).

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  SOIL WATER STATUS & IRRIGATION TRIGGER           |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Moisture Level    | Volumetric Water Content (VWC) Status         |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Field Capacity    | 100% Available Water Capacity (Stop irrigating)|
| Trigger Point     | 50% Readily Available Water (RAW) - START WATER |
| Permanent Wilting | Pasture stressed; severe yield loss occurs     |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+


### Scheduling Best Practices

* **Capacitance Probes:** Install soil moisture probes at multiple depths (**15 cm, 30 cm, and 60 cm**) within the primary pasture root zone to track daily water uptake.
* **Daily Evapotranspiration ($ET_c$):** Calculate daily water loss ($ET_c = ET_0 \times K_c$) using local weather station data and ryegrass crop coefficients ($K_c \approx 1.05$) to match application depth precisely to water loss.

---

## 3. Variable Rate Irrigation (VRI) & Application Uniformity

Modern center pivots equipped with Variable Rate Irrigation (VRI) technology adjust water application rates along individual span nozzles, skipping non-grazed areas such as raceways, trees, and watercourses.

* **Uniformity Testing (Catch Can Test):** Conduct periodic catch-can tests to verify the Christiansen Uniformity Coefficient (CUC). A CUC score **>85%** is required to prevent "striped" growth patterns.
* **Nozzle Maintenance:** Inspect drop nozzles regularly for wear, pressure regulator failure, or mineral clogging from hard ground water.

---

## 4. Runoff Prevention & Power Cost Optimization

Applying water faster than the soil’s saturated hydraulic conductivity causes surface ponding, soil erosion, and muddy tracks.

* **Match Application to Soil Type:** Heavy clay soils require lower application rates (**<5 mm/hour**) over longer cycles, whereas coarse sandy soils benefit from lighter, more frequent applications (**5–8 mm per pass**).
* **Off-Peak Energy Usage:** Run pumping stations during off-peak electrical tariff hours (typically nighttime or weekends) to minimize pumping energy costs while reducing water loss from daytime wind drift and evaporation.

Matching irrigation application to soil moisture capacity ensures water is converted efficiently into high-energy pasture biomass.
