# Herd Biosecurity, Pathogen Containment & Quarantine Protocols

Maintaining high herd health status requires stringent biosecurity measures that prevent the entry and spread of infectious diseases—such as Bovine Viral Diarrhea (BVD), Johne's Disease, Leptospirosis, and Mycoplasma bovis. Implementing clear quarantine zones, vehicle disinfection points, and stock testing protocols safeguards cattle welfare and protects long-term farm profitability.

---

## 1. Boundary Control & External Perimeter Security

Physical boundaries form the first line of defense against disease transmission from neighboring farms or straying stock.

[ Outer Perimeter Fence ] ---> [ Buffer Zone (3–5m) ] ---> [ Inner Herd Fence ] ---> [ Vehicle Decontamination Point ]


### Perimeter Security Benchmarks

* **Double Fencing:** Maintain a **3- to 5-meter buffer zone** along property boundaries shared with neighboring stock to eliminate nose-to-nose contact and saliva transfer.
* **Visitor Access Management:** Direct all visitors, service technicians, and contractors to a central point. Maintain a visitor logbook recording recent farm visits and contact history.
* **Boot Wash & Clean Protective Gear:** Require all visitors to use footbaths containing broad-spectrum disinfectants (e.g., Virkon) and wear clean farm-provided boots and overalls before entering animal housing or milking areas.

---

## 2. Incoming Stock Quarantine & Diagnostic Screening

Introducing new cattle (bulls, replacement heifers, or dry cows) represents the highest biosecurity risk for introducing new pathogens into a clean herd.

| Stage | Operational Standard | Testing / Action Required |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-Purchase** | Screen animal health history | Verify negative herd status for BVD, Johne's, and Leptospirosis |
| **Arrival Isolation** | Hold in designated quarantine facility | Isolate for a minimum of **21 to 28 days** away from main herd |
| **Diagnostic Panel** | Conduct blood, milk, or hair testing | PCR testing for BVD antigen/antibody and bulk testing for *M. bovis* |
| **Prophylactic Care** | Administer core treatments | Drench for internal/external parasites and administer booster vaccines |

---

## 3. Vehicle & Equipment Sanitation Protocols

Agricultural vehicles, livestock trailers, and service equipment can carry infectious pathogens across farm boundaries.

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  VEHICLE SANITATION CHECKLIST                     |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Vehicle Type      | Decontamination Requirement                   |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Stock Trucks      | Pressure-wash and disinfect crates before entry|
| Milk Tankers      | Restrict access to designated hardstand routes|
| Shared Machinery  | Clean off mud/manure prior to entering paddocks|
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+


---

## 4. Sick Pen Isolation & Internal Disease Containment

Containing disease within the farm requires isolating sick or compromised animals away from production lines and youngstock.

* **Dedicated Isolation Pens:** Maintain designated sick pens downwind and downstream from main housing areas, equipped with separate feeding and watering hardware.
* **Milking Sequence Order:** Always milk clinical or treated cows **last** to prevent pathogen transfer through milking clusters, or use a completely separate dump line system.

Enforcing strict perimeter controls, rigorous quarantine screening, and disciplined sanitation routines reduces disease risk and maintains herd productivity.
