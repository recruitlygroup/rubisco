## 1. High-Efficiency Parallel Parlour Operations (Double-20 to Double-50+)

Parallel parlours (cows positioned at $90^\circ$ perpendicular to the pit) are a staple of North American large-herd operations. Operators handle rapid sequencing while maintaining strict prep protocols to drive clean milk letdown and prevent cross-contamination.

[ Group Entry & Indexing ] ---> [ Strip, Dip & Wipe Routine ] ---> [ Rear Attachment ] ---> [ Automatic Detach ] ---> [ Rapid Exit Gate ]


### Candidate Performance Benchmarks for North American Parlours

* **Standardized Prep Routine:** Mastering the 4-step prep protocol—forestripping for clinical mastitis, applying pre-dip (minimum 30-second contact time), wiping thoroughly with individual microfiber cloths, and rear-mounting milking units.
* **Prep-to-Touch Timing:** Enforcing a strict **$60–90\text{ second}$** lag time between first teat stimulation and unit attachment to maximize natural oxytocin response and milk flow rates.
* **Rapid Exit Management:** Operating pneumatic gang gates and rapid-exit systems safely to clear milking rows in seconds and maintain high herd turnover per hour.

## 2. Headlock Operations, Fresh Cow Protocols & Herd Health

Lockup stanchions (headlocks) positioned along feed bunks are central to North American herd monitoring, AI breeding programs, and veterinary treatments.

| Practical Task | Student Execution Protocol | US/Canadian Farm Operational Value |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bunk Lockup Operations** | Manages mechanical gang-locks after fresh feed delivery to secure cows during post-milking lockup windows | Allows efficient herd health checks, vaccinations, and pregnancy diagnosis without isolating cows |
| **Fresh Cow Screening** | Conducts daily 1–10 day fresh cow checks: rectal temperature monitoring, ketone testing, and rumen fill scoring | Early detection of metritis, ketosis, and displaced abomasum (DA) to reduce involuntary culling |
| **Locomotion & Udder Health** | Scores mobility (1–5 scale) and identifies clinical mastitis grades (1–3) directly at the feed line | Enables rapid intervention and treatment logging before yield drops occur |
