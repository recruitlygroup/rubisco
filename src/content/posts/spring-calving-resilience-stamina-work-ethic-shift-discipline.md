# Spring Calving Resilience: Training Workforce Stamina, Work Ethic & Shift Discipline

Spring calving is the most intensive period on seasonal commercial dairy farms. Over a compressed 6- to 8-week window, farm teams face round-the-clock calving interventions, newborn calf pickups, colostrum administration, and rapid herd transitions into the milking parlour. The physical and mental demands test workforce endurance, reliability, and attention to detail. At CTEVT-affiliated agricultural training centers, candidate preparation extends beyond technical knowledge to instill physical stamina, mental resilience, and disciplined shift routines essential for seasonal peak periods.

---

## 1. Preparing for the Physical Demands of Peak Calving

Navigating mud, handling heavy newborn calves, operating outdoor break-fences in wet weather, and standing for multi-hour milking shifts require strong physical conditioning and operational discipline.

### Candidate Readiness Standards

* **Shift Endurance:** Practical training simulates real farm shift schedules, preparing students for early morning starts (**04:30 AM**) and sustained physical activity throughout multi-hour milking and feeding routines.
* **Weather Resilience:** Students complete outdoor modules under varying seasonal weather conditions, developing the habit of maintaining high work standards regardless of rain, wind, or cold temperatures.
* **Safe Manual Handling:** Candidates are trained in ergonomic lifting techniques for moving newborn calves, handling heavy feed bags, and positioning equipment to prevent back strain and fatigue-related injuries.

## 2. Rotational Night-Watch & 24/7 Calving Management

Managing heavy calving blocks requires systematic monitoring without compromising worker alertness during daytime operations.

| Operational Task | Student Execution Protocol | Employer Risk Mitigation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Night-Watch Inspections** | Conducts quiet 30- to 45-minute paddock/housing checks to monitor springing cows | Identifies dystocia early, reducing calf mortality and cow uterine injuries |
| **Dystocia Triage** | Recognizes the 3 stages of labor and knows when to intervene vs. when to allow natural progress | Prevents premature or aggressive assistance that harms cows |
| **Shift Handover Logs** | Maintains clear written records of cows showing signs of labor, recent calvings, and newborn feeds | Prevents missed colostrum feeds or duplicate interventions between shifts |
