# Pasture Irrigation Management, Water Use Efficiency & Soil Moisture Dynamics

Efficient water management is critical for maintaining consistent pasture production during dry seasonal windows. Applying the right depth of water at the precise time maximizes dry matter production per millimeter of water applied while preventing soil saturation, nutrient leaching, and energy waste.

---

## 1. Understanding Soil Moisture Metrics & Trigger Points

Effective irrigation scheduling requires tracking soil water availability within the primary root zone (0–300 mm depth).

[ Field Capacity (100%) ] ---> [ Stress Trigger Point (50% RAW) ] ---> [ Permanent Wilting Point (0%) ]
### Key Moisture Benchmarks

* **Field Capacity (FC):** The maximum depth of water soil can retain after excess gravitational water drains away.
* **Readily Available Water (RAW):** The fraction of stored soil moisture that plants extract easily without experiencing yield-reducing stress.
* **Trigger Point:** Apply irrigation before soil moisture drops below **50% of RAW** to prevent plant stomatal closure and growth slowdowns.
* **Soil Moisture Deficit (SMD):** The depth of water ($\text{mm}$) required to restore soil back to Field Capacity.

## 2. Irrigation Hardware Comparison & Application Rates

Selecting and calibrating application equipment ensures water delivery matches the infiltration capacity of different soil types.

| System Type | Application Rate | Best Suitability | Key Efficiency Factor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Center Pivot / Lateral** | High uniformity, adjustable depth (**3–10 mm**) | Flat to gently rolling pasture blocks | Drop nozzles to reduce wind drift loss |
| **K-Line / Pod Systems** | Low application rate (**2–4 mm/hour**) | Rolling topography, heavy soils | Long soak times reduce surface runoff |
| **Traveling Guns** | Variable, high depth (**10–25 mm**) | Long rectangular paddocks | High pressure requirements; prone to drift |

## 3. Water Scheduling & Weather Integration Protocols

Irrigation must adapt dynamically to daily Evapotranspiration ($\text{ET}_o$) rates and forecasted rainfall.

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+|                  DAILY IRRIGATION BALANCING ACT                   |+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+| Input / Output    | Operational Measurement                       |+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+| Loss ($\text{ET}_o$) | Daily moisture loss driven by heat & wind    || Gain (Rainfall)   | Effective rain recorded by on-farm weather stations || Net Irrigation    | Target Depth = Daily $\text{ET}_o$ - Effective Rain |+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
### Step-by-Step Daily Scheduling Routine

1. **Check Soil Probes:** Review capacitance probe telemetry or manual soil tension readings at **100 mm, 300 mm, and 600 mm** depths.
2. **Calculate Daily Balance:**
   `New SMD = Previous SMD + Daily ET - Effective Rainfall`
3. **Set Depth Target:** Apply a depth slightly less than the current deficit (e.g., if SMD is 15 mm, apply **10–12 mm**) to leave a buffer for unexpected rainfall.
4. **Avoid Deep Drainage:** Confirm that moisture increases occur at the 100 mm and 300 mm root depth sensors, but **not below 600 mm**, which indicates water and nutrient leaching below the root zone.

---

## 4. Hardware Maintenance & Energy Optimization

System maintenance preserves application uniformity across every span and nozzle.

* **Nozzle Inspection:** Replace worn or clogged drop nozzles before every irrigation season to maintain design distribution profiles.
* **Leak Reduction:** Check main line valves, hydrants, and tow hoses weekly; minor leaks waste energy and cause localized soil compaction.
* **Off-Peak Pumping:** Utilize off-peak electricity tariffs for pumping where possible to reduce operating overhead per hectare.

By matching application depths to root zone deficits and keeping hardware properly calibrated, farm teams conserve water resources, optimize electricity costs, and keep pasture yields consistent.
