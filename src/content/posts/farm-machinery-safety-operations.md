# Farm Machinery Safety Operations, Equipment Maintenance & Hazard Elimination

Agricultural environments present unique operational risks due to the constant interaction between high-powered machinery, heavy livestock, and unpredictable terrain. Implementing strict machinery operating procedures and risk mitigation frameworks protects farm workers, reduces costly equipment downtime, and maintains safety compliance.

---

## 1. Power Take-Off (PTO) Safety & Guarding Protocols

Power Take-Off (PTO) shafts deliver high rotational energy from tractors to implements, spinning between **540 to 1,000 RPM**. Entanglement hazards are severe and can occur in fractions of a second if safety shielding is compromised.

### Mandatory PTO Inspection Standards

* **Master Shield Integrity:** Ensure the tractor’s master shield is present, structurally sound, and correctly positioned over the output shaft.
* **Complete Guard Sleeve:** The PTO drive shaft must be fully enclosed in a plastic guard sleeve that spins independently of the inner shaft.
* **Restraining Chains:** Secure anti-rotation chains at both ends to fixed tractor and implement anchor points to prevent the outer guard from rotating.
* **Clearance Distance:** Maintain a strict **1.5-meter exclusion zone** around active PTO shafts during operation.

## 2. Tractor Operations & Heavy Implement Handling

Tractor stability depends on maintaining proper balance, understanding center-of-gravity shifts, and respecting load limits during transport and field maneuvers.

### Critical Operating Guidelines

1. **Roll-Over Protective Structures (ROPS):**
   * Never operate a tractor without a certified ROPS frame or cab.
   * Always fasten seatbelts when operating inside a ROPS-equipped vehicle to keep the driver within the safety zone during a rollover.

2. **Weight Distribution & Ballasting:**
   * Adjust front ballast weights when operating heavy rear-mounted implements (such as heavy silage grabs or subsoilers) to prevent front-end lifting.
   * Maintain at least **20% of total vehicle weight** on the front axle for effective steering control.

3. **Slope Handling:**
   * Drive straight up and down steep inclines rather than diagonally across slopes to prevent lateral tipping.
   * Keep heavy loads low to the ground while moving to preserve the tractor's low center of gravity.

## 3. Quad Bike & All-Terrain Vehicle (ATV) Stability Protocols

Quad bikes and ATVs are essential daily workhorses on pasture farms, but improper operation remains a leading cause of agricultural injuries.

| Feature | Standard Operating Requirement |
| :--- | :--- |
| **PPE** | Certified helmet (NZS 8608 / EN 1078) mandatory |
| **Crush Protection** | Fitted with certified OPD / Roll Bar |
| **Active Riding** | Shift body weight dynamically on uneven slope |
| **Towing Capacity** | Never exceed manufacturer tow limits (1:4 ratio) |

### Essential ATV Rules

* **Single-Operator Rule:** Never carry passengers on single-seat quad bikes; passenger weight alters the center of gravity and severely limits steering response.
* **Speed Management:** Keep speed below **20 km/h** on farm tracks, and reduce speed further when carrying spray tanks or trailed equipment.
* **Tire Pressure Calibration:** Check tire pressures weekly—uneven pressure between left and right tires leads to sudden steering pull and destabilizes the vehicle on turns.

## 4. Routine Maintenance & Pre-Start Inspections

Preventive maintenance identifies mechanical defects before they result in equipment failure or field accidents.

### Daily Pre-Start Checklist (The "POWER" Walkaround)

1. **P - Petrol/Fuel & Fluids:** Inspect hydraulic lines, engine oil, engine coolant, and fuel levels for leaks or deterioration.
2. **O - Operational Controls:** Verify brake responsiveness, steering play, clutch clearance, and throttle return springs.
3. **W - Wheels & Tires:** Check for tread wear, sidewall cracks, rim damage, and correct inflation pressures.
4. **E - Electrical & Lights:** Ensure work lights, indicators, flashing amber beacons, and brake lights are clean and functioning.
5. **R - Retainers & Hitch Pins:** Confirm trailer pins are secured with active linchpins or safety clips before transport.

---

## 5. Risk Assessment & Hazard Elimination Workflows

Systematic hazard control follows a clear **Hierarchy of Controls** to address farm risks directly at the source.

* **Elimination:** Remove dangerous machinery or bypass high-risk manual tasks completely where automated solutions exist.
* **Engineering Controls:** Install physical safety barriers, emergency kill-switches, slip-resistant steps, and guardrails around high-risk zones.
* **Administrative Procedures:** Implement clear Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), safety briefings, and mandatory logbook tracking for machinery faults.
* **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Enforce steel-cap boots, high-visibility vests, hearing protection, and eye safety gear across all farm operations.

Enforcing these operational standards protects farm personnel, extends equipment longevity, and maintains high compliance across modern agricultural workplaces.
