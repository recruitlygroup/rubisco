# Winter Housing Management, Cubicle Hygiene & Ventilation Protocols

Transitioning cows from pasture to winter housing alters environmental pathogen pressure, air quality, and animal movement dynamics. Implementing strict cubicle sanitation, maintaining optimal ventilation exchange rates, and avoiding overstocking are critical to preventing environmental mastitis (*Streptococcus uberis*, *E. coli*) and respiratory illnesses during indoor housing periods.

---

## 1. Cubicle Dimensions & Bedding Management

Proper cubicle design ensures cows lie down comfortably without injuring their limbs, soiling the bed, or causing teat damage to adjacent animals.

### Essential Cubicle Parameters

* **Space Allocation:** Provide at least **1 cubicle per cow** (aim for 1.05 to 1.1 cubicles per cow to reduce social competition).
* **Bedding Application:** Apply fresh lime, sawdust, or dry wood shavings to cubicle beds **twice daily** post-milking. Hydrated lime elevates bed pH above **9.0**, inhibiting bacterial growth.
* **Scraping Frequency:** Scrape cubicle heels and passageways clean at least **2 to 3 times daily** (or run automatic scraper alley blades continuously).

---

## 2. Barn Ventilation & Moisture Control

Inadequate airflow creates high humidity, ammonia buildup, and lingering moisture—providing an ideal environment for respiratory pathogens.

| Ventilation Metric | Target Parameter | Visual / Physical Check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Air Exchange Rate** | Minimum **4 to 8 air changes per hour** in winter | No lingering ammonia smell at cow head level |
| **Relative Humidity** | Keep below **75% to 80%** | No condensation dripping from roof sheets |
| **Inlet Area** | **0.1 m² per cow** minimum sidewall opening | Continuous air movement without high-velocity drafts |

---

## 3. Feed Face Space & Water Access

Restricted access to feed or water increases herd stress, drives social aggression, and drops daily dry matter intake.

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                    BARN INFRASTRUCTURE STANDARDS                  |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Resource          | Standard Operating Requirement                |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Feed Barrier Space| 0.6 to 0.75 meters per cow at total TMR face  |
| Water Troughs     | 10 cm trough length per cow; tipped/cleaned weekly|
| Passageway Width  | 3.0 meters minimum between cubicle rows       |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+


### Managing Feed Hygiene

* **Daily Push-Ups:** Push feed up **4 to 6 times daily** to encourage continuous intake and keep rations within comfortable reach.
* **Clean Surfaces:** Scrape and clear old, rejected feed daily before applying fresh total mixed rations (TMR) or silage.

---

## 4. Footbath Integration in Housed Systems

Indoor concrete surfaces accelerate hoof wear and increase exposure to slurry bacteria, making digital dermatitis management vital during housing.

* **Regular Passages:** Run cows through a **2%–5% Copper Sulfate or Formalin footbath** 3 to 4 times weekly as they exit the parlour.
* **Slurry Scraping:** Ensure passage scrapers operate smoothly without creating high "waves" of slurry that splash above the hoof hair line.

Maintaining clean cubicle beds, controlling barn air moisture, and protecting feed access allows cows to thrive indoors while safeguarding milk quality throughout the winter months.
