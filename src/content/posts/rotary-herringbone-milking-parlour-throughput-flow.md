# Automated Rotary & Herringbone Milking Parlour Operations, Throughput & Flow Management

Maximizing parlour throughput—measured in cows milked per hour—requires balancing gentle, stress-free stock movement with precise, mechanical routine execution. Whether operating a modern rotary or a swing-over herringbone system, maintaining smooth cow flow, optimizing platform speeds, and ensuring proper cluster alignment directly reduces total milking time while preserving udder health.

---

## 1. Cow Entry, Exit & Race Infrastructure Dynamics

Smooth cow movement into and out of the parlour eliminates entry delays and keeps cluster attachment rates consistent.

[ Backing Gate (Gentle Pressure) ] ---> [ Straight Entry Race ] ---> [ Rapid-Exit / Rotary Deck ] ---> [ Rubber-Mat Exit Race ]


### Key Structural & Flow Parameters

* **Backing Gate Operation:** Use backing gates solely to nudge space, never to push or crowd stock tight against entry doors. Excessive pressure causes stress, defaecation, and delayed milk let-down.
* **Entry Race Alignment:** Ensure entry lanes feature solid side walls to prevent visual distractions, keeping floor transitions level to encourage confident stepping.
* **Exit Lane Width:** Exit races must be wide enough (**1.5 to 1.8 meters** for single lanes, or rapid-exit gates for herringbone layouts) to prevent bottlenecks as cows leave the milking area.

---

## 2. Rotary Platform Speed & Operator Sequence Optimization

In rotary parlors, throughput relies on aligning platform rotation time with average herd milking duration.

| Operational Variable | Recommended Target Benchmark | Adjustment Trigger / Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rotation Speed** | **8 to 11 minutes** per full revolution | If cows hit exit without cluster removal, slow rotation slightly |
| **Entry Stop Sensor** | Automated beam pause | Stops deck if a cow hesitates, preventing missed stalls |
| **Milking Routine Sequence** | Strip $\rightarrow$ Pre-Dip $\rightarrow$ Wipe $\rightarrow$ Attach (**60–90 sec lag**) | Ensures optimal oxytocin release before cluster attachment |

---

## 3. Herringbone Batch Management & Swing-Over Efficiency

Herringbone parlours rely on synchronized batching, where entry efficiency depends on the slowest-milking cow in the row.

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                   HERRINGBONE ROUTINE WORKFLOW                    |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Routine Step      | Operational Execution                         |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| 1. Entry & Prep   | Allow full side to load; prep first 3–4 cows   |
| 2. Attachment     | Attach clusters sequentially down the pit row |
| 3. Exit Trigger   | Open rapid-exit gates as soon as line finishes|
| 4. Next Batch     | Guide next herd batch in immediately          |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+


### Minimizing Idle Time

* **Swing-Over Units:** Position clusters on high-line tracks so operators can move units directly across the pit without twisting hose lines.
* **Automatic Cluster Removers (ACRs):** Set ACR takeoff thresholds correctly (**0.2 to 0.4 kg/min flow rate**) to prevent over-milking and teat end damage (*hyperkeratosis*).

---

## 4. Vacuum Dynamics & Cluster Alignment Checks

Proper hose alignment during attachment prevents liner squawk, slip, and uneven quarter evacuation.

* **Long Air Hose & Milk Tube Alignment:** Align the cluster directly under the cow's udder, ensuring long milk tubes hang straight without twisting or pulling the claw sideways.
* **Vacuum Level Targets:** Maintain stable claw vacuum between **38 to 42 kPa** during peak milk flow to avoid udder congestion and tissue stress.

By streamlining cow entry protocols, matching rotary speeds to milking duration, and maintaining correct cluster alignment, farm personnel maximize parlour throughput while maintaining low teat-end injury rates.
