# Automated Heat Detection, Oestrus Synchronization & Reproductive Management

Achieving high reproductive performance is essential for maintaining seasonal calving patterns and maximizing milk production across the herd. Integrating automated activity monitoring technology with structured oestrus synchronization protocols allows farm managers to optimize submission rates, improve first-service conception rates, and shorten the empty period.

---

## 1. Visual vs. Sensor-Based Heat Detection

Relying solely on visual observation for heat detection often leads to missed standing heats, particularly during night hours or high-stress periods.

[ Activity Spike / Rumination Drop ] ---> [ System Heat Alert ] ---> [ Optimum AI Timing Window ]


### Comparative Detection Benchmarks

| Detection Method | Efficiency Rate | Key Indicators & Parameters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Visual Observation** | 45% – 55% | Standing to be mounted, chin resting, clear mucus discharge |
| **Activity Collars / Tags** | 80% – 90%+ | High locomotion index combined with a sharp drop in rumination minutes |
| **Tail Paint / Scratch Cards** | 65% – 75% | Friction rubbing, color change, or paint removal from tailhead |

---

## 2. Optimizing the Artificial Insemination (AI) Window

The timing of artificial insemination relative to the onset of standing heat directly impacts fertilization success.

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                    OPTIMUM INSEMINATION TIMELINE                  |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Onset of Heat     | Zero Hour (Activity spike detected)           |
| Optimum Window    | 12 to 18 hours after initial activity surge   |
| Ovulation         | ~24 to 30 hours post-onset of heat            |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+


### Execution Best Practices

* **The AM/PM Rule:** Cows identified in heat in the morning are inseminated late afternoon; cows identified in heat in the evening are inseminated the following morning.
* **Sperm Viability:** Frozen straw thawing must occur in a temperature-controlled water bath at **35°C–37°C for exactly 30 seconds** prior to gun loading.

---

## 3. Oestrus Synchronization Protocols

Synchronization programs help bring non-evident, silent, or delayed-cycling cows into heat predictably, ensuring tight calving windows.

### Standard Fixed-Time AI (FTAI) Workflow (e.g., Ovsynch)

1. **Day 0:** Administer **GnRH** injection to induce ovulation or luteinization of dominant follicles.
2. **Day 7:** Administer **Prostaglandin ($PGF_{2\alpha}$)** to lyse the active corpus luteum.
3. **Day 9:** Administer second **GnRH** injection to trigger timed ovulation.
4. **Day 10 (16–20 hours post-GnRH):** Perform Fixed-Time Artificial Insemination regardless of visual heat signs.

---

## 4. Non-Return Rate Tracking & Pregnancy Diagnosis

Maintaining detailed reproductive records ensures problem cows are flagged early for veterinary intervention.

* **21-Day Submission Rate Target:** Aim for **>75% to 80%** of the target herd submitted for insemination within the first 3 weeks of the breeding season.
* **Pregnancy Scanning:** Conduct transrectal ultrasound scanning at **35 to 50 days post-AI** to confirm pregnancy status, determine fetal age, and identify non-cycling anestrous cows requiring therapy.

By pairing automated activity sensors with disciplined AI handling and structured synchronization, farm teams raise herd submission rates, reduce open days, and safeguard seasonal herd profitability.
