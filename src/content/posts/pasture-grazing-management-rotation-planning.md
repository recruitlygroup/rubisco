# Pasture Grazing Management, Rotation Planning & Feed Allocation

In pasture-based dairy systems, effective pasture management is the single biggest driver of farm profitability. Controlling rotation length, accurately measuring covers, and hitting target post-grazing residuals ensures cows receive consistent Metabolisable Energy (ME) while maintaining long-term pasture quality and pasture species persistence.

---

## 1. The Rye-Grass Leaf Stage Principle & Pre-Grazing Targets

Allocating pasture based on plant biology ensures maximum energy density ($>11.5\text{ MJ ME/kg DM}$) and prevents pasture quality degradation.

[ 1-Leaf Stage (Root Recovery) ] ---> [ 2-Leaf Stage (Energy Storage) ] ---> [ 2.5 to 3-Leaf Stage (OPTIMUM HARVEST) ]


### Key Pasture Benchmarks

* **Optimum Pre-Grazing Cover:** Target **2,800 to 3,200 kg DM/ha** (or 2.5 to 3 leaves per ryegrass tiller). Grazing beyond the 3-leaf stage leads to canopy shading, dead matter accumulation at the base, and declining crude protein.
* **Target Post-Grazing Residual:** Aim for **1,500 to 1,600 kg DM/ha** (roughly **3.5–4.0 cm** or 7–8 compressed clicks on a plate meter). Consistent residual control forces plants to tilt back into leafy regrowth rather than stem elongation.

---

## 2. Calculating Daily Pasture Allocation & Feed Deficits

Accurately matching herd dry matter demand to available pasture cover prevents under-feeding and under-grazing.

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                    FEED ALLOCATION CALCULATION                    |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Total Daily Herd Demand = (No. of Cows) x (Target DM Intake/Cow)  |
|                                                                   |
| Pasture Allocated = Area (ha) x (Pre-Grazing Cover - Residual Target)|
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+


### Step-by-Step Allocation Example

1. **Herd Demand:** $300\text{ cows} \times 18\text{ kg DM/cow/day} = 5,400\text{ kg DM total daily demand}$.
2. **Pasture Yield Available:** Pre-grazing cover of $3,000\text{ kg DM/ha}$ minus target residual of $1,500\text{ kg DM/ha} = 1,500\text{ kg DM/ha}$ consumable grass.
3. **Daily Area Required:** $5,400\text{ kg DM} \div 1,500\text{ kg DM/ha} = 3.6\text{ ha/day}$ (allocated across 12-hour or 24-hour strip breaks).

---

## 3. Managing Seasonal Surplus & Deficit Windows

Adjusting rotation speed maintains feed quality across changing growth rates throughout the season.

| Season / Condition | Growth Rate | Target Rotation Length | Strategic Management Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Spring Flush** | Very High ($>70\text{ kg DM/ha/day}$) | Fast (**18–21 days**) | Drop surplus paddocks out of rotation for high-quality silage |
| **Summer Dry** | Moderate to Low ($<35\text{ kg DM/ha/day}$) | Extended (**30–40 days**) | Fill feed deficit using silage, brassicas, or partial TMR |
| **Autumn Rounding** | Declining ($30–40\text{ kg DM/ha/day}$) | Slowing (**30–60 days**) | Build average farm cover (AFC) prior to dry-off and winter |

---

## 4. Break-Fencing & Strip Grazing Execution

Strategic break-fencing improves grazing utilization and prevents stock from walking back and damaging recent regrowth.

* **Back-Fencing:** Always run a back-fence on 24-hour and multi-day allocations to prevent cows from re-grazing fresh regrowth tillers, which drains plant root energy reserves.
* **Water Trough Access:** Position portable water troughs or central lane access gates so stock can drink without breaching back-fenced areas.

By enforcing rigid pre-grazing targets, calculating exact allocation areas, and maintaining residual consistency, farm managers convert pasture growth directly into high-value milk solids.
