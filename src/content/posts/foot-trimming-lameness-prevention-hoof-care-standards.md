# Foot Trimming & Lameness Prevention: Hoof Care Standards for Commercial Dairy Herds

Lameness is one of the most costly health conditions affecting commercial dairy farms, directly reducing milk yield, impairing reproductive performance, and increasing premature culling rates. Whether cows are walking long distances on pasture tracks or standing on concrete in indoor housing facilities, claw lesions—such as sole ulcers, white line disease, and digital dermatitis—severely disrupt cow welfare and herd profitability.

At CTEVT-affiliated agricultural training centers, candidate instruction focuses on early locomotion scoring, functional hoof trimming according to international standards, and proactive preventative footbath management.

---

## 1. Locomotion Scoring: Early Identification of Gait Abnormalities

Detecting lameness at Score 2 (mildly lame) rather than Score 4 or 5 prevents permanent structural damage to the claw and accelerates recovery times.

[ Locomotion Scoring (1–5 Scale) ] ---> [ Crush Restraint & Inspection ] ---> [ Functional 5-Step Trim ] ---> [ Block / Bandage Application ]


### Locomotion Scoring Standards

* **Daily Walking Observation:** Candidates observe cows walking on flat, non-slip surfaces post-milking, assessing back-arch posture and stride length.
* **Early Triage System:** Cows exhibiting an arched spine while walking (Score 2) or while standing (Score 3) are drafted for hoof inspection within 24 hours.

---

## 2. Functional Hoof Trimming: The Dutch 5-Step Method

CTEVT candidates pass practical exams proving safe crush restraint, angle measurement, and disciplined execution of functional trimming steps using manual knives and electric grinders.

| Trimming Step | Execution Protocol | Biomechanical Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1: Toe Length** | Measures wall length to **75–80 mm**; trims inner claw sole thickness to 5 mm | Restores correct weight-bearing length on medial claw |
| **Step 2: Match Outer Claw** | Trims outer claw wall length and sole thickness to match inner claw | Equalizes weight distribution across both claws |
| **Step 3: Model the Sole** | Dings out a concave dish in the axial sole area of both claws | Relieves pressure on the sensitive sole ulcer site |
| **Step 4: Relieve Lesion** | Lowers the affected claw if a lesion is present | Shifts weight-bearing load onto the healthy claw |
| **Step 5: Remove Loose Horn** | Pairs away loose wall horn, necrotic tissue, and heel cracks | Eliminates anaerobic micro-environments for bacteria |

## 3. Preventative Footbath Chemical Management

Automatic and manual footbaths prevent infectious hoof conditions like digital dermatitis (Mortellaro) when chemical concentration and volume are strictly controlled.

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|               FOOTBATH MAINTENANCE & DOSING STANDARDS              |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Parameter         | Operational Standard Requirement              |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Dimensions        | Minimum 3.0m length (ensures 2 dips per hoof)|
| Chemical Concentration | 2–5% Copper Sulfate or 3–5% Formalin solution |
| Pass Frequency    | 200–250 cow passes maximum before solution dump|
| Pre-Rinse Wash    | Water bath prior to chemical bath to strip mud|
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
