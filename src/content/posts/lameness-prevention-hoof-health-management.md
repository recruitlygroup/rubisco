# Lameness Prevention, Hoof Health & Mobility Management in Dairy Cattle

Lameness is one of the most costly health issues in pasture-based and housed dairy systems, directly causing reduced dry matter intake, drop in milk yield, and lower conception rates. Implementing routine mobility scoring, maintaining farm infrastructure, and establishing standard hoof-trimming procedures keeps cows mobile, healthy, and productive.

---

## 1. Locomotion & Mobility Scoring Systems

Detecting early structural gait changes allows farm personnel to treat soft tissue and claw lesions before severe tissue damage occurs.

[ Weekly Mobility Walk ] ---> [ Identify Score 2/3 Cows ] ---> [ Isolate in Hoof Crush ] ---> [ Functional Trim & Block ]


### Locomotion Scoring Framework (0–3 Scale)

* **Score 0 (Clear/Normal):** Walks with an even stride and a flat back during both standing and walking.
* **Score 1 (Mildly Impaired):** Unequal weight distribution or slight back arching while walking, but walking speed remains normal.
* **Score 2 (Lame):** Short strides on one or more legs with a pronounced back arch both standing and walking; immediate intervention required.
* **Score 3 (Severely Lame):** Extreme difficulty moving; unable to keep up with the herd; requires urgent therapeutic trimming and rest paddocks.

## 2. Common Hoof Lesions & Therapeutic Treatments

Accurately diagnosing claw diseases ensures correct treatment, whether addressing infectious skin conditions or non-infectious mechanical structural breakdowns.

| Condition | Primary Cause | Visual / Physical Symptoms | Immediate Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sole Ulcer** | Excessive pressure on claw corium (hard surfaces) | Raw red lesion on the sole bulb area | Trim weight off affected claw; apply wooden/rubber block to healthy claw |
| **White Line Disease** | Shearing forces on tracks; gravel penetration | Separation of outer wall from sole with dirt packing | Carefully pare out trap line; drain pus; relieve weight bearing |
| **Digital Dermatitis** | Bacterial infection (*Treponema*) in moist conditions | Painful, red, strawberry-like lesion on heel skin | Clean and dry lesion; apply topical antibiotic spray or bandage |

## 3. Infrastructure & Track Management Best Practices

Poorly maintained farm raceways and parlour exit zones are primary drivers of mechanical claw injuries.

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                    TRACK & RACEWAY DESIGN STANDARDS               |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Feature           | Standard Specification                        |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Surface Layer     | Fine clay/sand mix capped over coarse aggregate|
| Crossfall / Crown | 3% to 5% slope to shed surface water rapidly  |
| Entry / Exit Zones| Install heavy-duty rubber matting on concrete |
| Herd Flow Speed   | Never rush cows; allow them to choose head height|
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+


### Stockmanship Rules During Movement

* **Avoid Pressure:** Never force cows tightly using backing gates or dogs; crowding forces animals to step on sharp stones or pivot awkwardly on concrete.
* **Maintain Patience:** Allow the herd to move at its natural walking pace (2 to 3 km/h), keeping their heads down to see foot placement on tracks.

---

## 4. Footbath Design & Chemical Application Protocols

Footbathing prevents contagious hoof infections across the entire herd when integrated into regular weekly milking routines.

* **Dimensions:** Ensure the bath is at least **3 meters long**, 0.6 meters wide, and 15 cm deep so each hoof receives a minimum of **two full immersions** per pass.
* **Solution Options:**
  * **Copper Sulfate (2%–5%):** Hardens horn tissue and controls digital dermatitis.
  * **Formalin (2%–5%):** Highly effective disinfectant (use strictly in open-air, well-ventilated exit lanes).
* **Refresh Frequency:** Change footbath solutions every **200 to 250 cow passes** to prevent organic matter from deactivating the chemicals.

Enforcing weekly mobility checks, optimizing track surfaces, and executing prompt therapeutic trimming keeps herd lameness rates below **5%**, protecting animal welfare and daily milk output.
