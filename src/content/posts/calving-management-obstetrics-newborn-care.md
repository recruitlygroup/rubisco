# Calving Management, Obstetrics & Immediate Newborn Calf Resuscitation

The calving season is the operational focal point of the dairy calendar. Proper calving supervision, timely intervention during difficult births (dystocia), and immediate post-birth resuscitation safeguard cow health, protect future fertility, and maximize calf survival rates.

---

## 1. Recognizing the Stages of Labor & Intervention Timelines

Knowing when to assist versus when to allow natural progress prevents unnecessary tissue trauma to the cow and stress to the calf.

[ Stage 1: Cervical Dilation (2–6 Hours) ] ---> [ Stage 2: Fetal Expulsion (1–2 Hours) ] ---> [ Stage 3: Placental Discharge (<12 Hours) ]


### Stage Breakdown & Action Triggers

* **Stage 1 (Dilation):** Restlessness, isolation, nesting behavior, and tail-raising. Allow **2 to 6 hours**.
* **Stage 2 (Active Straining):** Appearance of the water bag or hooves.
  * *Heifers:* Allow up to **2 hours** of continuous progress.
  * *Cows:* Progress should occur within **1 hour** of active abdominal straining.
* **Intervention Rule:** Examine the cow if there is **no progress 30 minutes** after the water bag ruptures or after active straining begins, or if the calf presents abnormally (e.g., tail-first or head-turned).

---

## 2. Obstetric Hygiene & Calving Assistance Standards

Incorrect traction or unhygienic intervention leads to uterine tears, nerve paralysis, and severe uterine infections.

| Step | Standard Operating Procedure | Equipment / Material |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sanitation** | Wash vulva and tail with warm water and antiseptic solution; wear fresh shoulder-length gloves | Chlorhexidine / Iodine wash, plastic sleeves |
| **Lubrication** | Apply generous obstetric lubricant inside the vagina and around the calf's head/limbs | Non-irritating veterinary lube |
| **Rope Placement** | Secure calving ropes with a double hitch: primary loop **above the fetlock**, secondary loop **below the fetlock** | Clean nylon calving ropes |
| **Traction Angle** | Pull only when the cow strains; pull **straight out** until the chest passes, then **downward toward the hocks** | Manual traction or calibrated calving jack |

---

## 3. Newborn Calf Resuscitation Protocols

Immediate care post-delivery clears airway mucus, stimulates breathing, and prevents hypothermia.

+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  NEWBORN RESUSCITATION STEPS                      |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Action            | Standard Procedure                            |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Position          | Place calf in "Sitting" position (sternal recumbency)|
| Airway Clearance  | Clear straw/mucus from mouth; DO NOT hang upside down|
| Breathing Trigger | Insert clean straw inside nose or pour cold water on head|
| Umbilical Dip     | Submerge navel cord in 7% Iodine solution immediately|
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------+


### Critical Resuscitation Rules

* **Avoid Hanging Upside Down:** Hanging calves over gates forces abdominal organs onto the diaphragm, making lung expansion harder and causing fluids from the stomach—not lungs—to drain.
* **Thermal Care:** Move wet calves directly to a dry, draft-free pen with deep straw or under infrared heat lamps during cold weather.

---

## 4. Post-Calving Cow Monitoring

Inspect the dam immediately following delivery to identify complications before returning her to the transition group.

* **Check for Twins:** Re-examine the birth canal immediately post-delivery to confirm no second fetus remains.
* **Tear Inspection:** Check for vaginal or cervical tears and administer anti-inflammatories or antibiotics as advised by veterinary protocols.
* **Immediate Fluid Access:** Provide a warm water drench (**20–30 liters**) containing calcium and energy supplements to encourage immediate appetite and rehydration.

By enforcing strict intervention timers, practicing clean obstetrical assistance, and applying immediate resuscitation techniques, farm teams minimize calving losses and protect herd welfare.
