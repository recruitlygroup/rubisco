# Automated Drafting Gates, RFID Herd Identification & Precision Sorting Systems

Managing large herds efficiently requires individual animal attention without slowing down overall cow flow or adding unnecessary labor. Integrating Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) ear tags with automated directional drafting systems allows farm personnel to automatically sort target animals—such as cows in heat, animals due for dry-off, or stock requiring veterinary treatment—as they exit the milking parlour.

---

## 1. RFID Hardware Setup & Electronic Identification Dynamics

Precision sorting relies on rapid, error-free reading of Half-Duplex (HDX) or Full-Duplex (FDX) Electronic Identification (EID) ear tags under high-speed cow movement.

[ RFID Ear Tag ] ---> [ Panel Antenna Reader ] ---> [ Management Controller ] ---> [ Pneumatic Gate Actuator ]


### Critical Hardware Parameters

* **Antenna Positioning:** Mount panel antennas at eye level along a narrow, single-file exit race before the directional gate junction to prevent multi-tag signal overlap.
* **Tag Read Distance:** Calibrate readers to achieve a **>99.5% read rate** at cow movement speeds of up to **2.0 meters per second**.
* **Electrical Noise Suppression:** Ensure variable speed drives (VSDs) on nearby milk pumps or backing gates are properly shielded and grounded to eliminate electromagnetic interference (EMI) with RFID signals.

---

## 2. Automated Drafting Sequences & Pneumatic Gate Mechanics

The sorting junction uses fast-acting pneumatic air cylinders to direct cattle into designated holding yards, AI handling races, or return lane pastures without interrupting herd momentum.

| System Component | Operational Standard | Preventative Maintenance Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Air Compressor Pressure** | Maintain stable supply at **6.0 – 8.0 bar** | Drain water condensation trap daily; inspect airline seals |
| **Pneumatic Ram Speed** | Cushion speed setting (<1.0 second gate transition) | Adjust end-stroke cushioning valve to prevent gate slamming |
| **Photo-Eye Sensors** | Positioned to detect cow chest and tail clearance | Clean optical lenses weekly to prevent dust and manure blinding |

## 4. Drafting Yard Design & Animal Welfare Standards

Holding pens for drafted cows must minimize animal stress while stock await artificial insemination, hoof trimming, or veterinary examination.

* **Space Allocation:** Provide at least **2.5 to 3.0 m² per cow** in temporary holding yards with nonslip rubber matting on concrete floors.
* **Water & Shade Access:** Install accessible water troughs in all holding pens to maintain hydration during extended sorting periods.
* **Smooth Return Lanes:** Ensure non-drafted cows have a clear, unimpeded exit path back to main pasture tracks to prevent bottlenecks at the drafting gate exit.

Automating animal identification and sorting eliminates manual drafting errors, saves operator hours, and ensures time-sensitive treatments are applied precisely when needed.
